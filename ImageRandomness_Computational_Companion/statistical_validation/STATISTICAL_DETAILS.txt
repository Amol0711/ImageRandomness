Statistical details and recorded evidence
Finite-grid calibration, paired detection and window sensitivity

Purpose and reading order

This note documents statistical protocols and numerical diagnostics.
It connects each result to
the complete observations, calculations and verification programs.
Paths below are relative to statistical_validation/ unless stated otherwise.
Reported rates and interval endpoints in this note are percentages, whereas
the CSV and JSON records store probabilities as fractions. Differences
between rejection probabilities are reported in percentage points.

1. Null models and sampling design

The Gaussian-calibration study contains 27 cases and 525,000 independent
fields. Each case has 20,000 fields except the 256 by 256 random-image pair,
which has 5,000. The design covers alphabets 2, 16, 256 and 65536, small
and large grids, rectangular windows, strips and full-image windows.
Fixed references include constant, checkerboard, split, palette and diverse
configurations. Checkerboard and split references use intensities 0 and q/2.
The deterministic palette and diverse constructions are specified in
../moment_computation/COMPUTATIONAL_DETAILS.txt and results/protocol.json.

Two further models specify independent difference pixels on {0,...,7}.
The first has probability vector

    p = (1/2, 1/8, 1/8, 1/16, 1/16, 1/16, 1/32, 1/32).

The sparse law has p_s(0)=1023/1024 and p_s(7)=1/1024, with zero mass
elsewhere. Their exact moments use the specified probability laws.

Every two-sided Gaussian rule uses the exact null mean. The exact-variance
rule uses V=Var(S). The pair-occurrence rule instead uses

    V_pair = sum_e c_e Var(h_e),     h_e = abs(D_i-D_j),

where c_e is the comparison multiplicity. This rule omits the squared
multiplicities and shared-endpoint covariances of the exact variance.
At nominal level alpha, its normal-tail prediction is

    2 Phi(-z_(1-alpha/2) / sqrt(V/V_pair)).

Here Phi is the standard normal distribution function and z is its quantile.
This prediction describes the effect of the variance ratio under a normal
approximation. It is separate from the observed rejection proportion.
Rectangular windows and specified-law cases assess finite-grid behavior.
The single full-image window is a stress test with one statistic per
independent field.

results/calibration.json and calibration.csv retain every case at nominal
5%, 1% and 0.1%, with separate lower and upper tails, empirical moments,
moment ratios and quantiles. results/calibration_predictions.json and
calibration_predictions.csv independently reconstruct twelve specified configurations. Their 95% Wilson intervals are pointwise
confidence intervals for the exact-variance rule's rejection probability.

The fixed diverse reference's exact-variance interval is [0.741%, 0.998%],

The complete twelve-row table also retains the binary, rectangular,

The binary 4 by 4 grid with 2 by 2 windows has observed rejection 1.610%
at nominal 1%. At nominal 5%, the lower and upper tails are 4.695% and
0.040%, respectively, giving total 4.735%. The total near 5% therefore
coexists with pronounced tail asymmetry.

For the sparse 16 by 16 case, the empirical variance is 0.9922 times its
exact value. The exact-variance rule rejects 2.585%, 2.510% and 1.655%
at nominal 5%, 1% and 0.1%, respectively. Every observed rejection is in
the upper tail. At nominal 1%, the pair-occurrence normal-tail prediction
is 44.138%, whereas its observed rejection is 17.320%. Accurate variance
normalization and accurate Gaussian tail probabilities are distinct issues.

At nominal 0.1%, attaining that level would produce an expected 20
rejections in an ordinary 20,000-field case and five in the largest-grid
case. The finite-null evaluations below quantify the binary and sparse
departures independently of Monte Carlo variation.

3. Exact and certified rejection probabilities

Complete enumeration of all 65,536 binary 4 by 4 difference fields gives
exact rejection probabilities for the existing Gaussian rules. For the
sparse law, enumeration covers all 177,589,057 supports containing at most
four nonzero pixels. Every support retains its original probability.
The exact omitted probability is Pr(Binomial(256,1/1024)>4), approximately
6.38217463693e-6, and is added once to obtain the certified upper bound.
This corresponds to approximately 0.000638217464 percentage points.

1                 1.544189          [2.43948, 2.44013]
0.1               0.518799          [1.60529, 1.60594]

Binary values are displayed to six decimal places. Sparse endpoints are
rounded outward to five decimal places. At nominal 5%, the exact binary
lower and upper tails are 4.43115234375% and 0.0457763671875%, respectively.
Sparse rejection occurs entirely in the upper tail at all three levels.
These evaluations quantify the existing Gaussian rules rather than define
new exact-level tests. Integer rejection sets and truncation bounds are evaluated by
analysis/exact_calibration.py. results/exact_calibration.json
retains exact rational weights, score histograms, quantile certificates,
source hashes and all verification outputs.

4. Rank calibration and the realized null bank

The paired experiment uses 64 by 64 grids with q=256 and six raw statistics.
They are the NPCR change count, UACI absolute-difference sum, and weighted
pair sums S_w for square-window widths w=2,3,4,5. Centered discrepancies
use their exact null means. A separate bank of 99,999 null fields sets the
randomized rank thresholds at nominal 5% and 1%. All six statistics observe
the same field in each model. Independent uniform tie variables resolve
equal discrepancies, and each statistic reuses its ties across significance
levels to preserve nested rejection sets.

A separate 20,000-field null sample assesses the realized bank's conditional
rejection probabilities. The six observed rates range from 4.950% to 5.140%
at nominal 5% and from 0.945% to 1.050% at nominal 1%. All twelve pointwise
95% Wilson intervals contain the corresponding nominal level. The exact
common marginal levels average over banks and new observations. A realized
bank has its own conditional rejection probabilities. The central 95%
intervals from the across-bank Beta law are [4.866%, 5.136%] and
[0.939%, 1.063%], respectively. These are intervals describing variation
across independent banks, distinct from confidence intervals conditional
on the bank used in the experiment.

The NPCR count has a binomial law under the independent null and partial
identity. Evaluating the exact-law expression in float64, including the
randomized boundary mass, gives conditional null rejection probabilities
4.955055% and 0.985270%. Their observed values are 4.950% and 0.945%.
For partial identity with epsilon=0.005, the nominal 5% probability is
98.5937%, compared with observed rejection 98.540%.

For disjoint copying, the zero-count generating function gives the NPCR
law at the realized bank threshold. At theta=0.10, nominal 5% rejection
probabilities are 6.0704% for lag one and 5.9999% for lag three, compared
with observed values 6.70% and 5.30%. The observations retain these
finite-sample deviations from the conditional model probabilities.
Independent reconstruction reproduces every saved NPCR and UACI sequence
across 199,999 fields, including the bank. Exact small-field enumeration
also verifies the copying generator. The detailed probabilities, boundary
values and pointwise diagnostics are in results/npcr_copying_diagnostic.json
and results/audit.json.

5. Paired detection and graphical display

Each of the eight nonzero alternatives uses 10,000 independent fields.
Disjoint copying preserves the individual difference-pixel law while
changing joint laws. Partial identity replaces individual differences by
zero independently and changes the pixel-equality probability. These
models induce complementary responses in pair differences and equality
counts. results/power_rates.csv retains all six statistics under every
model at both significance levels, with pointwise Wilson intervals.

At nominal 5%, lag-one copying with theta=0.10 gives rejection 35.89%
for S_2 and 6.70% for NPCR. Lag-three copying with theta=0.30 gives
11.37% for S_4 and 7.86% for S_2. Partial identity with epsilon=0.005
gives 6.20% for S_2 and 98.54% for NPCR. At epsilon=0.010, NPCR rejects
all 10,000 simulated observations, giving the observed proportion 100%.
This remains a finite-sample estimate of population power.

The three selected paired contrasts at nominal 5% are

Alternative                   First minus second  Difference (pp)   95% interval (pp)
Lag-one copy, theta=0.10       S_2 minus NPCR            29.19           [27.66, 30.70]
Lag-three copy, theta=0.30     S_4 minus S_2              3.51           [2.59, 4.43]
Identity, epsilon=0.005        S_2 minus NPCR           -92.34           [-92.97, -91.64]

Here pp means percentage points. Every contrast uses 10,000 common fields.
Its interval combines two 97.5% Clopper-Pearson intervals for the discordant
probabilities, giving at least 95% pointwise coverage conditional on the
bank. The coverage statement applies separately to each contrast.
results/power_paired.csv retains the complete paired results.

The complete six-statistic comparison, including lag-three copying, is
retained in results/power_rates.csv. The independent null assessment uses
20,000 fields and each nonzero-alternative case uses 10,000 fields.

6. Finite-boundary window sensitivity and mean checks

The exact window study evaluates 220 mean-sensitivity indices over widths
2 through 12, alphabets q=2 and q=256, square grids 64 by 64 and 256 by
256, and lags 1,2,3,4,6. The copying probability is theta=0.10. The index
is the exact mean decrease divided by the null standard deviation.

For q=256 and lag three, selected indices are

Grid       Width 4        Width 5
64x64      0.161994938    0.161985405
256x256    0.669931283    0.675179590

The finite boundary changes the maximizing width within this search from
four on the smaller grid to five on the larger grid. On the smaller grid,
the observed nominal 5% powers at theta=0.30 are nearly tied at 11.37%
and 11.30%. The different theta values identify separate sensitivity and
power evaluations. Maximizing a mean-sensitivity index and maximizing
rejection probability are different objectives.

All 54 empirical means across the null and eight alternative models lie
within 2.61 estimated Monte Carlo standard errors of their exact predictions.
For lag-three copying at theta=0.30, S_2 and S_3 have zero exact mean
response but observed rejection rates 7.86% and 8.47%. Their null rates
are 5.09% and 5.08%, respectively. These are descriptive comparisons of
observed rejection despite zero exact mean shift. The complete sensitivity
grid and exact moment inputs are retained in results/sensitivity.json and
sensitivity.csv, and empirical mean diagnostics are in results/power.json.

7. Verification and evidence map

The saved-data checks reconstruct calibration tail counts, rank thresholds,
rejection rates and paired contrasts from recorded integer scores and seeded
ties. results/power_audit.json reports 12 reconstructed thresholds, 108
rejection-rate records and 198 paired contrasts, with all checks passed.
The checks verify nested decisions across significance levels and retain
the exact integer discrepancy keys. Independent finite-model checks cover
scoring, moments, copying, partial identity and rank exchangeability.
These checks are documented in results/verification.json and results/audit.json.

Public export identity

The companion-root EXPORT_PROVENANCE.json distinguishes original experimental
digests from distributed-file digests. Descriptive metadata and local paths
are normalized; numerical observations and computational parameters retain
their original values. Public checksums must not be interpreted as original
experimental file checksums.
