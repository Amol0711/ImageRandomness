Computational details and recorded evidence
Exact moment computation for image randomness statistics

Purpose and reading order

This note documents the computational protocols and recorded observations.
It brings together the auxiliary timing, allocation and sampling results,
while the machine-readable records retain every recorded repetition, exact
output, resource decision and source checksum. Paths in this note are
relative to moment_computation/ unless explicitly stated otherwise.

The principal moment-computation comparison uses the 18-case
scaling study in results/scaling.json. Its five-point area curve uses only
that study. The complete six-degree vertex curve uses the separate 60-call
batch in results/vertex_benchmark.json. The original 23-case study remains
available in results/comparison.json and supplies the auxiliary comparisons
below. These are distinct experiments with different resource rules and
execution orders. Their repetitions and medians are never pooled, and time
ratios use unrounded medians from the same experiment.

Section 6 gives the complete principal benchmark protocol, execution
conditions and completion ledger. Section 9 supplies its numerical tables.
The principal 18-case study covers alphabet size, image area and window width.
The prepared vertex kernels use the separate complete experiment in Section 7.

1. Common computational model and implementations

For a deterministic reference v, independent pixels U_i are uniform on
{0,...,q-1}, D_i = abs(v_i-U_i), and the raw statistic is

    S = sum_{(i,j,c) in edges} c * abs(D_i-D_j).

The four implementations evaluate the same raw mean and variance formulas.
Exact breakpoint is the deterministic uncached algorithm, and Cached
breakpoint adds profile and pair caching. Both use arbitrary-precision
integers. Exact alphabet evaluates finite sums with integer prefix sums.
These three methods return exact rational moments. Float64 alphabet
evaluates the finite-sum formulas in NumPy using the occurring folded-law
classes and unordered edge-class pairs, and returns floating-point
approximations. All four retain shared-endpoint covariance by aggregating
incident projections. Their recorded method names are piecewise_exact,
piecewise_cached, alphabet_exact and alphabet_float64, respectively.

For zero-based row-major pixel index r, the deterministic construction uses

    t = (1664525*(r+271828)+1013904223) mod 2^32,
    x = t xor floor(t/2^13).

Here xor denotes bitwise exclusive OR. Palette references set k = x mod 32
and v = floor(k*(q-1)/31). Diverse references set v = x mod q. Checkerboard
references alternate 0 and floor(q/2) according to row-plus-column parity.
Split references use 0 in columns below floor(columns/2) and floor(q/2)
elsewhere. The protocol files record each case's geometry and construction.

Per-reference timings include reference validation, profile and pair
preparation, vertex accumulation and materialization of both outputs.
Reference generation and prepared graph construction are outside these
timings. Caches are rebuilt on every call. Graph enumeration and graph
validation/adjacency construction have separate measurements. Ordinary
automatic garbage collection remains enabled, with an explicit collection
before each measured call and outside the timer.

The recorded moment studies use an AMD EPYC 9V74 processor, Linux x86-64,
Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5. Calls run sequentially with one numerical
backend thread. The shared host is not reserved exclusively. Elapsed times
characterize these implementations, inputs and recorded execution conditions.

2. Independent verification

results/verification.json records exact covariance-expansion checks on
1,004 fixed-reference graphs, including 100 signed-weight cases. Exact
alphabet evaluation agrees on all 1,004 graphs, and 902 also undergo full
comparison-image enumeration. Additional checks cover 405 rectangular
geometries and 58 general rational-law instances. All exact comparisons
pass. The record also retains zero-variance, input-validation, graph-reuse,
normalization and large-alphabet structural checks.

The vertex-kernel verifier in code/vertex_ablation.py independently compares
both aggregation kernels with direct alphabet enumeration on 182 small
cases, including signed weights and exact cancellation. These untimed
algorithm checks are separate from saved-record audits. The latter verify
the arithmetic, provenance and completeness of existing measurements
without rerunning timing experiments.

3. Original 23-case timing study

The original study covers grids from 4 by 5 to 256 by 256, alphabets
2, 256, 4096 and 65536, and rectangular, strip, square and full-image
windows. The four methods use three fresh repetitions in the recorded
listed order. The complete design is results/protocol.json, and all 92
case-method rows are retained in results/comparison.json and comparison.csv.

The alphabet work proxy is q*(2*e+K_f+C_f), where e counts distinct edges,
C_f counts occurring folded-law classes and K_f counts occurring unordered
class pairs. The limits are 2,000,000 for Exact alphabet and 200,000,000
for Float64 alphabet, with a 128 MiB estimated workspace guard. The
original runner additionally applies a 300,000-edge eligibility check to
Exact breakpoint. The work proxy is a prespecified resource rule, not a
measured operation count. A resource omission supplies no elapsed time and
does not represent a timeout or a measured speedup.

The study completes 69 case-method combinations and records 23 omissions.
All four completed Exact alphabet comparisons agree exactly. All 19
Float64 alphabet comparisons meet the 1e-10 relative variance-error target.
Their maximum absolute relative variance error is 1.3408600710130832e-16.

5. Replicated Monte Carlo moment estimation

The simulation comparison uses the original area_16 and area_64 inputs,
with q=256, palette references and 2 by 2 windows. At each sample count
1,000, 5,000 and 20,000, ten independent replications estimate the raw
mean and variance. The variance estimator is the unbiased sample variance
with ddof=1. Relative errors use the exact variance as reference. Empirical
relative root mean squared error (RMSE) is calculated across the ten
replications. Each descriptive 95% interval uses 10,000 bootstrap
resamples of those replications and their 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles.

Grid    Samples  Relative variance RMSE (%)  Descriptive 95% interval (%)  Median seconds
16x16      1000            5.889                    [3.729, 7.541]            0.006897
16x16      5000            1.627                    [0.572, 2.451]            0.019763
16x16     20000            1.129                    [0.634, 1.527]            0.080789
64x64      1000            4.190                    [2.852, 5.306]            0.142000
64x64      5000            1.937                    [1.471, 2.297]            0.734463
64x64     20000            0.808                    [0.452, 1.102]            2.986892

The sampling timer includes random-number generation, difference formation,
weighted edge statistics and sample mean/variance calculation. It excludes
graph/reference construction and exact oracle calculation. At 20,000
samples on the larger grid, the median is 2.986891574 s, compared with
0.162822684 s for Cached breakpoint and 0.086777145 s for Float64 alphabet
in the original timing study. These comparisons quantify observed sampling
error alongside computation cost. Both 20,000-sample bootstrap upper
endpoints exceed 1%, so the recorded experiment does not establish a
sample budget delivering 1% population accuracy. The intervals summarize
uncertainty across ten replications and do not certify a precision target.

The master simulation seed is 2026102501. Each replication uses
numpy.random.SeedSequence([master_seed, case_index, sample_size,
replicate_index]), with zero-based indices. Bootstrap seeds derive from
[2026102502, case_index, sample_size]. The batch size is 32 and the edge
chunk size is 8192. All 60 individual runs, signed errors, exact oracles
and summaries are retained. These moment-estimation experiments are
separate from the rejection-rate and detection studies in
../statistical_validation/.

6. Principal 18-case benchmark protocol

The seven degree cases use a 32 by 32 palette grid at q=256 with square
window widths 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16. The six alphabet cases use a
16 by 16 palette grid and 2 by 2 windows at q=2^8, 2^12, 2^16, 2^20,
2^24 and 2^32. Three additional area cases use 64 by 64, 128 by 128 and
256 by 256 palette grids at q=256 with 2 by 2 windows. Two diverse
32 by 32 references use q=256 and q=4096.

The five-point area series reuses alphabet_q2p8_16 and
degree_w2_32 for its 16 by 16 and 32 by 32 points, followed by area_64,
area_128 and area_256. All five points come from results/scaling.json.
The original study's area cases have similar names but separate records.

The 18 distinct inputs have the following case ledger. The two small-grid
points reused in the area curve do not add cases to the design.

Group                 Cases  Reference
Window widths             7  palette
Alphabet sizes            6  palette
Additional image areas    3  palette
Reference diversity       2  diverse

Palette inputs use 32 integer intensity levels distributed across the
alphabet by the deterministic formula in Section 1. Diverse inputs allow
all q levels through the same fixed integer construction. Both assign the
values deterministically to pixel positions. Folding may change the number
of distinct local laws as q changes, so the records include actual folded
class and occurring class-pair counts.

The recorded host uses an AMD EPYC 9V74 processor, Linux x86-64, Python
3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5. Calls run sequentially with one numerical-backend
thread and no concurrent measurement jobs. The backend thread variables
are all set to one in the saved metadata. Each case uses three fresh
moment calls per eligible method. Repetition r, indexed from zero, rotates
the listed order Exact breakpoint, Cached breakpoint, Exact alphabet,
Float64 alphabet left by r positions. This rotation uses three of the four
possible starting positions. Caches are local to a call and rebuilt for
every repetition. A tiny untimed q=8 call per method precedes measurement
at each process invocation; there is no per-case warmup.

Elapsed wall-clock time includes reference validation, profile and pair
preparation, edge and vertex accumulation, and materialization of both
moment outputs. Reference generation, graph preparation, output comparison,
diagnostic serialization and checkpoint I/O are outside this timer.
Graph enumeration and validation/adjacency construction have separate
measurements, and prepared topology is reused. Explicit garbage collection
precedes each measured call outside the timer, while ordinary automatic
garbage collection remains enabled. A median, minimum and maximum are
reported only after all three repetitions complete.

The prespecified resource limits are 512 MiB of estimated additional
workspace for each alphabet method, a nominal 120 s per measured call,
and 300,000 distinct edges for every prepared comparison graph. One MiB
equals 2^20 bytes. The workspace estimate concerns working arrays and is
not a process-memory cap. This protocol has no alphabet work-proxy cutoff.
Resource omissions, censored timeouts and interrupted calls have distinct
statuses. Signal delivery may be deferred inside native calls, so actual
elapsed times are retained. Allocation tracing follows the timing phases.

The study completes 67 of 72 case-method combinations, comprising 201
timed calls. Five combinations exceed the estimated workspace guard.
No moment-timing call reaches the timeout. All 15 completed Exact
alphabet comparisons agree with both breakpoint implementations in all
repetitions. The 16 completed Float64 alphabet cases have maximum absolute
relative variance error 1.0297705359333676e-13, below the 1e-10 target.
These accuracy counts refer to this study alone.

Method                Completed cases  Workspace omissions
Exact breakpoint                   18                   0
Cached breakpoint                  18                   0
Exact alphabet                     15                   3
Float64 alphabet                   16                   2
Total                              67                   5

The five omissions are Exact alphabet at q=2^20, 2^24 and 2^32, and
Float64 alphabet at q=2^24 and 2^32, all on the 16 by 16 palette input
with 2 by 2 windows. They supply no elapsed time. The absence of a
moment-timing timeout applies to these 201 untraced calls. Section 4
separately records the timed-out Exact alphabet allocation trace at
q=2^16 and preserves its partial peak and elapsed time.

Independent covariance expansion and exhaustive small-case enumeration
check the implementations separately from timing comparisons. Section 2
lists those verification records. In the principal benchmark, exact-output
equality and floating-point error are assessed against the completed
uncached breakpoint result, preserving exact numerator and denominator
strings. Float64 outputs are converted to their exact binary-rational
values before errors are calculated. This keeps exact agreement distinct
from the measured accuracy of the floating-point implementation.

The saved records include edge and degree counts, folded classes, occurring
class pairs, event/interval counts, and times divided by the edge count
and by n+e+sum_i d_i*log2(1+d_i). Here n is the number of vertices and
d_i is vertex i's degree. Event and interval counts describe structural
work, not CPU instructions. Recorded bit lengths describe unreduced final
mean and variance numerators, not the largest intermediate operand.

7. Complete vertex comparison and historical record

The complete vertex-only experiment uses degrees 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 and
256 at q=65536. The center reference is floor(q/3), leaf j has reference
floor(j*(q-1)/(2*(d+1))) for j=1,...,d, and all incident weights equal
one. Leaf folded radii are mutually distinct. Each kernel receives the
same prepared profiles and calculates the same central-vertex raw
second-moment sum. The sweep aggregates incident profiles before squaring.
The pairwise kernel integrates all diagonal and unordered cross products.

Five fresh repetitions per method alternate execution order. All 60 calls
complete with exact agreement. Profile preparation, explicit garbage
collection and result serialization are outside the timed kernels.
Automatic garbage collection remains enabled, and allocation tracing is
absent from the timings. Every recorded call is retained, including the
degree-256 sweep maximum of 0.003388705 s.

At degree 256, the sweep processes 773 event records and integrates 344
intervals. The pairwise expansion handles 32,896 products and 185,984
intervals. The medians are 0.0012799280011677183 s and
0.7806834999937564 s, respectively, with ratio
609.9432931239214. This is a prepared vertex-kernel comparison. The
complete moment baselines already use projection aggregation, so this
ratio does not describe complete moment-algorithm runtime.

results/vertex_verification.json records 1,602 saved-record consistency
and structural checks, with all checks passed and no timing rerun.
results/vertex_benchmark.json and vertex_benchmark.csv supply all six
degrees and their within-experiment vertex ratios.

The older vertex record remains in results/vertex_scaling.csv and the
vertex_cases object of results/scaling.json. All 18 moment cases had
completed before a duplicate-key error during vertex CSV export
interrupted the first degree-eight sweep call. That call has no saved
elapsed time or output and is recorded as censored_interruption. Its
remaining sweep repetitions were suppressed. The degree-eight pairwise
kernel and both kernels at the other five degrees completed after the
CSV-export correction, leaving 55 completed calls in that historical record.

provenance/run_scaling_moment_record.py preserves the measured moment
runner, and provenance/scaling_interruption.json preserves its checkpoint
before the CSV-export correction. The final scaling record retains its initial
metadata and documents the source transition. The moment algorithms,
input design and protocol bytes were unchanged, and original moment
timings were retained without repetition. The separate complete vertex
batch supplies a full six-degree comparison. Historical vertex calls
are neither spliced into that curve nor pooled with its medians.

and 3,891 occurring pairs leave little reuse among 3,906 edges. Cached
breakpoint takes 0.2270 s, compared with 0.2188 s without caching, while
Exact alphabet and Float64 alphabet take 6.296 and 0.1726 s. Caching is
therefore input-dependent. Small alphabets can favor compact exact or
vectorized alphabet evaluation, while larger alphabets increase the work
and storage of enumeration.
